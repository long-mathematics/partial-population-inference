# Research continuation

The central objective remains a transfer-and-generation theory: state which experiment and target are given, generate calibrated statistics from the Poisson additive transform where applicable, and identify what survives transfer.

## 1. Certified projected intervals

Implement optimization of the joint convex regions over positive measures, first on fixed finite grids with explicit approximation error. A grid optimizer alone is not a continuum certificate. Develop dual inequalities with interval arithmetic or an analytic sign certificate, and compare relaxed solutions with integer-realizable populations.

Evaluate a preassigned mixture of effort-optimized full-count and fixed-effort deletion calibration. The continuation theorem optimizes normalizers for a common score; arbitrary data-selected directions or maxima of full-deletion e-values require separate justification.

Deliverable: reproducible missing-mass and fixed-horizon novelty intervals, with independently checked coverage calculations on finite populations and certified optimization error.

## 2. Matched comparisons and one real dataset

Choose one documented ecological or vocabulary dataset with a stable source and permissible redistribution. Preserve the original data and transformation script. Specify sampling design before selecting a benchmark. Use simulation from known populations to assess coverage and width; one real dataset does not reveal repeated-sampling coverage.

Compare richness estimates on a common minimum-mass class with Chao and Wu–Yang as appropriate. Compare prediction with smoothed Good–Toulmin and Eriksson under their stated regimes. Treat individual unseen probability and maximum unseen probability as distinct targets when discussing Painsky and Colombi et al. Do not compare point-estimator error bounds with confidence-interval width without constructing a matching inferential task.

## 3. Tag–retag and inverse coupon

Recover and version the original exact tag–retag work before claiming its inclusion. Define finite-population sampling with or without replacement, tagged-set selection, detection heterogeneity, and the stopping rule separately.

Derive the exact observation kernel in each design. Determine which positive transfers preserve the target, whether dominated transfer improves calibration, and whether stopping changes the relevant law. Seek a sharp bounded-heterogeneity inverse-coupon interval with a finite-parameter or moment dual certificate. Do not infer validity at stopping from a fixed-time e-value.

## 4. Structural limits and manuscript consolidation

Keep the two existing drafts intact until the combined theorem/notation map is settled. A combined exposition should proceed from experiments and transfer kernels to the additive transform, measure geometry, calibration, and dual certificates. The tank game belongs as a finite decision example; it is not a Poisson species-factorization corollary.

Connect bounded-batch fluctuation work after recovering its current version. Overlap changes the needed joint incidence data, and one-label extraction loses information about the original batches.

Before submission, replace literature-search impressions by explicit theorem-level comparisons. The moment-envelope and common-score conditioning arguments may have close antecedents; no priority conclusion follows from the searches completed so far.
