## Positioning

Good (1953), Good–Toulmin (1956), Chao (1984), Harris (1959), and Efron–Thisted (1976) remain the classical statistical lineage. The current drafts already cite them. Blackwell, Le Cam, and Torgersen supply the comparison-of-experiments framework; elementary transfer classifications should not be presented as foundational discoveries.

The strongest plausible contribution is the combination of explicit exponential-kernel recovery, simultaneous target envelopes, integer-realizability distinctions, and computable joint calibration across experiments. Establishing exactly which components are new requires a closer theorem-by-theorem comparison, especially with generalized moment theory and de-Poissonization.

The continuation note's common-score normalization is a direct conditioning-and-positivity argument. Its useful interaction with affine cumulant geometry is proved here, but the literature search does not establish that this principle or its specialized applications are new.

The supplied review also mentions an ICLR labeling application without identifying a paper. No specific citation has been inferred from that description; it remains a literature lead, not a verified source.

## Benchmark discipline

Record the target, sample design, population class, and coverage convention for every comparison. Point estimation of support, total missing mass, maximum unseen probability, and prediction of future discoveries answer different questions. Joint unconditional coverage is different from conditional prediction coverage. An empirical width comparison should only follow this alignment.

No third-party paper is copied into this repository; links identify the sources and the manuscript bibliographies carry the formal citations.
